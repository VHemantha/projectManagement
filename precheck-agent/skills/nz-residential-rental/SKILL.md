# Residential rental (NZ)

Long-term residential rental, per entity and per property. Development, property trading,
commercial or short-stay activity at scale is flagged to the preparer rather than forced through
this. Closed accounts, settled loans and sold properties are needed only up to closure. A 31 March
balance date is not assumed.

## Build the expected lists first

From last year's accounts and workpapers, list the bank accounts, loans and properties; then update
for what the questionnaire says changed this year. Every item on those lists needs evidence again.

## AFIT's request rules

- **Who manages each property** (from the questionnaire): property manager or owner. Map every
  property to its manager and the months managed; do not assume one manager covers everything.
- **Property manager**: request the annual summary (preferred) or every monthly statement — gross
  rent, fees, expenses paid by the manager. Avoid double counting with the net transfers in the bank.
- **Owner managed, or months without a manager**: request an expense summary; rent is taken from
  the bank statements, month by month — flag gaps.
- **Short-term rental (e.g. Airbnb)**: if a property manager runs it, request the manager's
  statements; otherwise request the platform's annual or monthly statements.
- **Last year's expenses**: for each expense line in last year's accounts, if it was not paid
  through a bank account or manager statement we hold, request the invoice. Body corporate fees:
  always request the levy invoice or statement (it shows the split, including any insurance and
  long-term maintenance fund) — a bank payment alone is not enough.
- **Bank accounts and loans**: every account and loan from last year again. If the questionnaire
  says a new bank account was opened or a loan refinanced, request the new account's statements
  and the refinancing documents (old loan closing details, interest to settlement, new loan).
  Per loan: annual summary showing interest for the full year, principal and closing balance —
  principal is not interest.
- **Rent bond**: request the bond details, and whether the bond was closed (refunded or kept for
  damage or arrears) during the year.
- **Annual meeting expenses**: if the questionnaire mentions an annual (e.g. body corporate or
  family) meeting, request the details — purpose, attendees, costs.
- **Payments over NZD 1,000**: a single transaction over $1,000 is assumed to relate to the
  property: request the invoice, so the preparer can decide repair or capital.
- **Property changes**: purchase or sale — the sale and purchase agreement and both parts of the
  settlement statement (property and finance); chattels list with values; subdivision information.
- **Rates**: council rates invoices or statements for every property and the full year; an
  assessment does not prove payment. **Insurance**: the policy or renewal for each property.
- **Owner costs**: home office (if any property is self-managed), vehicle kilometres for this year
  (never reuse last year's), phone, internet, stationery — accept an adequate schedule.
- **Accrued items**: last year's accrued accounting fees or prepaid insurance roll into this year.

## NZ rules the preparer will apply (ask for what they need; do not decide)

- Interest on residential rental loans: 80% deductible for the 2024-25 income year and fully
  deductible from 1 April 2025 — the loan annual summaries matter.
- Rental losses are ring-fenced (carried forward against future residential rental income).
- Bright-line test: 2 years for residential land sold on or after 1 July 2024 — a sale needs the
  purchase and sale dates and documents.
- Residential buildings are not depreciated; chattels are. Assets costing under $1,000 can be
  written off. New assets bought from 22 May 2025 may qualify for Investment Boost (not land or
  residential buildings) — flag, do not decide.
- Short-stay through a platform may involve GST and the mixed-use asset rules if the property is
  also used privately — flag for the preparer.
