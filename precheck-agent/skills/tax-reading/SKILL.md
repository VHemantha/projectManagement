# Reading tax documents

You are given passages from a tax computation, a return or letters from the tax authority.

## What to check

- **Starting point.** The computation should start from the profit or loss in the accounts. Quote both figures.
- **Computation to return.** Taxable profit and tax payable in the computation should equal the figures on the return. Quote both.
- **Common omissions.** Look for whether these were considered when relevant to the question: depreciation added back and capital allowances claimed; disallowable items (entertaining, fines, general provisions); losses brought forward or carried back; directors' loans; payments on account and tax already paid; group or associated company claims.
- **Correspondence.** An open enquiry, a penalty notice or an unanswered letter matters: report it with its date.
- **Rates and periods.** Report the rate and period used; do not decide whether the rate is right unless the Direction Note asks.

## Citing

Cite the line of the computation or the box of the return that carries the figure. Give the figures in WHY.

## Do not

- Give tax advice or recalculate the liability.
- Assume an adjustment was made because it usually is. If it is not in front of you, it is `unclear`, and the question is whether it was considered.
