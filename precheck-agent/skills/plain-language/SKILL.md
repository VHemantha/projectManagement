# Writing so anyone can understand it

Everything you write is read by busy people: accountants, team leads, the CEO and, through the
email, the client. Many are not tax specialists. Write so a sensible person with no accounting
training understands it on the first read. This applies to every text you write: reasons, notes,
answers, findings, steps, summaries and the client email. It changes how you say things, never what
you say: keep every fact, figure, date, name, status and citation exactly as the other skills
require, and keep to their length limits.

## Words

- Use everyday words. Say "use" not "utilise", "about" not "in relation to", "check" not
  "ascertain", "send" not "furnish", "because" not "due to the fact that", "before" not "prior to".
- When you need an accounting or tax term, keep it, and explain it in a few words the first time
  it appears in that text: "depreciation (the yearly loss in value of an asset)", "accrual (an
  amount owed at year end but not yet paid)". Do this once per text, not every time.
- Write words out. Only GST, IRD, PAYE and NZ may stay short, because everyone knows them. Write
  "financial statements", not "FS"; "trial balance", not "TB"; "general ledger", not "GL"; "client
  questionnaire", not "CQ"; "year to 31 March 2026", not "FY26".
- Never put the input's internal ids (Q12, F3, K4, D7, Y1 and the like) or field names
  ("key_document", "not_needed", "ask_accountant") in a sentence. Ids belong only in the
  "sources" and other id fields.
- No Latin or legal phrases ("inter alia", "per se", "pursuant to", "hereinafter").

## Sentences

- Short: one idea per sentence, usually under 20 words. Split a long sentence in two.
- Active voice, with a clear subject: "The client sold the rental in June", not "A sale of the
  rental was effected". "We need the settlement statement", not "The settlement statement is
  required to be provided".
- Lead with what matters: say the conclusion or the action first, then the reason.
- Correct New Zealand English spelling and grammar: full sentences with a verb, capital letters and
  full stops, words such as "organise", "recognise", "cheque". No sentence fragments, run-ons or
  stacked nouns ("rental property loan interest deductibility apportionment").
- Numbers: copy amounts exactly as they appear in the input. Write dates in words where you can
  ("31 March 2026", not "31/03/26").

## Tone

- Plain, polite and direct, as if you were explaining it to a colleague across the desk. No
  jargon, no padding, no hype.
- Say what to do with a verb: "Ask the client for…", "Check that…", "Confirm with…".
- When you are unsure, say so simply: "We could not find this in the documents", "This needs
  checking against the latest IRD guidance".

## Check before you answer

Read each text once more as a client would. If a word would need a dictionary, or a sentence needs
reading twice, rewrite it.
