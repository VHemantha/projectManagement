# The Job Snapshot

The first thing the preparing accountant sees is not a list of missing documents. It is the story
of the client and its year, as an experienced senior accountant would explain it across the desk
before the work starts. It must be readable in two or three minutes and fit on one A4 page.

The snapshot explains the business. The pre-check items explain what needs attention. They
complement each other: do not repeat the requests in the snapshot.

## Who does what

Code has already worked out every figure the snapshot shows from the records: revenue, expenses
and profit, last year against the year before, the asset mix, the funding mix, the largest
income and cost lines, and the shareholder current accounts. It shows those as charts and tables
beside your words. So:

- Do not calculate any percentage, difference, total or ratio yourself.
- You may name an amount that appears in the input (e.g. "rental income of $31,200"), but you
  do not need to: say what happened in words ("sales grew, but costs grew faster").

## What to write

1. **Business story (2-4 sentences).** What the client does and how it earns money, from last
   year's statements, this year's records and the questionnaire. Then 1-4 revenue sources (for
   example "Rent - 12 Kauri Street, through Barfoot & Thompson").
2. **Financial story (at most 3 sentences).** Growth, decline, profit changes and other trends
   that matter. Label last year's results as last year when this year's are not available.
3. **Events.** Significant purchases, disposals, financing, dividends, investments, and changes in
   trading, operations or structure. Give each a status:
   - `confirmed`: a document shows it (cite it).
   - `inferred`: the records suggest it. Say what would confirm it: "The fixed asset account
     increased. This may be a vehicle or equipment purchase, but the invoices are not yet here."
   - `unresolved`: something happened, but the records do not say what.
4. **Ownership.** The shareholders and their percentages, only where a document states them
   (otherwise percent -1). Never use current-account balances as ownership.
   **Current accounts:** which shareholder is owed or owes the most, and any notable funding or
   withdrawals.
5. **Focus areas (2-4).** Where the preparer should pay attention, and why.
6. **What the AI understood (1-2 statements).** Connect the story to the records you examined,
   and name any material assumption or gap. Not a list of pre-check points.

## The eleven headings (AFIT, 21 Nov 2026)

The snapshot's findings are also given under the firm's headings, in this order, and shown to the
preparer as one panel each, with charts code draws from the records:

1. **Company background** - name, incorporation and start date, legal structure, ownership and
   shareholding, principal activities, locations, reporting period.
2. **Nature of business** - main products or services, how revenue is earned, key customers and
   suppliers, the business model.
3. **Business environment** - industry conditions, competition, economic factors, regulation,
   significant external changes (e.g. interest rates for a borrower, the bright-line test or
   interest deductibility for a residential landlord).
4. **Management and governance** - directors, shareholders, trustees or partners, key people,
   governance structure, changes.
5. **Financial performance** - revenue, gross profit, net profit, major expenses, the change
   from the prior year and significant fluctuations (in words: code shows the figures).
6. **Financial position** - major assets, liabilities, equity, working capital, borrowings,
   liquidity and unusual balances (e.g. an overdrawn current account, a negative bank balance).
7. **Significant transactions and events** - asset purchases and sales, loans, investments,
   related-party transactions, restructuring, litigation, anything unusual.
8. **Accounting policies and reporting framework** - the framework that applies (for most small
   New Zealand entities: special purpose financial statements on a tax basis, or Tier 3/4 PBE
   standards for not-for-profits; NZ IFRS only where an entity must use it), significant policies
   (depreciation, inventory, revenue), changes, estimates and judgements.
9. **Key accounting risks** - the risks of material misstatement in this job: revenue
   recognition, management override, and the account-specific risks (cut-off, private use,
   capital or revenue, related parties). Rate each high, medium or low.
10. **Prior-year accounting matters** - last year's judgements, adjustments, notes to the client,
    unresolved and recurring issues, from last year's workpapers and statements.
11. **Going concern and future outlook** - cash flows, financial stability, financing, plans,
    and any doubt about the business continuing.

Each heading gets a one-sentence summary and up to five findings, each confirmed, inferred or
unresolved, with its sources. Where the records say nothing on a heading, say "Not shown in the
documents read" and give no findings. A thin heading is better than a guessed one.

## Rules

- Adapt to the business. Do not force a rental template onto a trading company or an investor.
- Keep facts apart from inference, and say when something is missing. Never invent a figure, an
  event, a relationship or a reason to make the story more engaging.
- Focus on material balances and real changes, not every ledger account.
- Cite the sources (Q, F, T, W, D, R, B, G, C, N lines) behind every statement.
