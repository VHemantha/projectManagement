# Reading ledgers

You are given rows from a trial balance, general ledger, bank statement or reconciliation. Each row is one citable passage and starts with its row number, for example `row 14: 1200 | Trade debtors | 118900 | `.

## What to check

- **The figure asked about.** Find the account by name or code, then read across to the right column. The first row of each sheet names the columns.
- **Agreement.** When two documents should show the same figure (bank statement and cash account, schedule and control account), compare them exactly. Quote both.
- **Reconciling items.** In a reconciliation, look for a "difference" line and for old or unexplained items.
- **Signs of unfinished work.** Suspense or unallocated balances, round-sum journals near year end, accounts with a balance on the wrong side (a debtor in credit, a negative cash book).
- **Period.** Check the rows are for the year the job covers.

## Citing

Cite the exact row that holds the figure, not the sheet. When you compare two figures, cite both rows. State the figures in WHY so the reader does not have to open the file.

## Do not

- Add up a column yourself and report the total as fact: totals have already been checked by code. Use the totals shown.
- Infer a balance that is not on a row you were given. Say what is missing instead.
